The big one

JPMorgan locks the vault on outside AI agents

Jamie Dimon and Mark Zuckerberg. Image: Getty Images via CNBC

On Tuesday's earnings call, Jamie Dimon said JPMorgan will not let outside artificial intelligence agents into customer accounts to act on their behalf. A spokeswoman told CNBC the bank "currently blocks external AI agents from operating on behalf of retail customers." Agents may still see some balances read-only through a connector that already holds the user's credentials. They cannot send money or pay a bill. There is no timeline for opening the door.

My read: the agent economy just hit its first locked vault, and it belongs to America's biggest bank. Dimon is not anti-AI. He is building the bank's own tool, Smart Cash, a pilot that predicts cash flow and moves money for yield, and keeping everyone else's agents out. Banking Dive quotes BofA's Ebrahim Poonawala on the real fight: whoever owns the agent owns the customer. "Liability transfer" is banker for "if your bot moves the money wrong, that is on you." Anyone running a stack that touches money should read that twice.

The catch: the block is not new policy so much as Dimon saying it out loud, with no date to reverse it. Smart Cash is still a pilot with no results published. The outside agents named include Meta's assistant and Spear Street Technology's text-message concierge.

By the numbers:

  • "Thousands": rogue agents Dimon warned there "could be."
  • $10 billion: Spear Street's valuation last month (CNBC).
  • ~$700 million: JPMorgan's annual AI spend, per CFO Barnum ("a little stale").
  • 12%: rise in quarterly expenses, to $27.3 billion.

Sources: CNBC · Banking Dive · FA Mag
