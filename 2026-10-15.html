The big one

A shot in the arm just switched off a brain gene

Tau is one of the proteins that clump in Alzheimer's brains. Image: Getty Images via BioPharma Dive

On Wednesday, Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals posted interim Phase 1/2a data for ARO-MAPT, an RNA-interference drug that silences the MAPT gene and cuts production of all six isoforms of tau. The delivery is the story: a subcutaneous injection, not a needle in the spine. Arrowhead calls it the first clinical validation of a platform that carries siRNA across the blood-brain barrier. The results are in healthy volunteers only.

My read: forget the Alzheimer's headline for a moment. Getting a gene-silencing drug into the brain used to mean spinal delivery. Arrowhead says it did it with a jab under the skin, and the effect held for three months after the last dose. If that holds up, it is a bloody doorway to every brain disease with a bad gene behind it.

The catch: a lower tau level in spinal fluid is not a slower decline in memory. Patient data are not expected until 2027. Tau drugs have a graveyard of trials behind them. The stock jumped premarket Wednesday; the close is unverified here.

By the numbers:

  • 73%: mean cut in CSF total tau at Day 105, top dose. Best individual: 85%.
  • 40% and 69%: mean cuts at the lower two doses.
  • 90 days: Day 105 is 90 days after the last dose; supports dosing every three months or less often.
  • ~64 healthy volunteers and up to 48 early-Alzheimer's patients: planned study size.

Sources: Arrowhead newsroom · BioPharma Dive · ClinicalTrials.gov
