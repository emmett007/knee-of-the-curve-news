Frontier labs testify under oath to the NYC Council today

Source: qz.com/…/nyc-council-ai-hearing (updated Oct 5)

Zombie cells hide from the cleanup crew, and a drug turned the crew back on

Albert Einstein College of Medicine, in Nature Aging today: as chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA), the cell's targeted recycling system, declines with age, senescent cells change what they secrete and macrophages get worse at engulfing them. The oral CMA activator CA77.1, given to aged mice for five months, cut senescent-cell burden across organs and eased a bleomycin lung-fibrosis model when started early. Lung lysosomes from human IPF patients showed the same CMA weakness.

Set it next to last week's dasatinib + quercetin biopsy result and you get two different bets. D+Q kills the cells directly. This one restores the body's own clearance.

Source: nature.com/articles/s43587-026-01240-w (Oct 5) · eurekalert.org release · drugtargetreview.com/…/cma-restoration

Aging's double failure: the trash piles up and the janitor goes blind

The Cuervo lab's paper ties two hallmarks of aging into one loop. CMA drops, senescent cells' secretions stop calling for cleanup, and those same secretions suppress CMA in the macrophages sent to clear them. CA77.1 pushed aged macrophages' engulfing back toward young levels. The commentator's job is to explain why "fix the recycling so the body clears them" is a different wager from intermittent kill protocols.

Why this handle: Longevity science with a mechanism, not a supplement pitch.

Live-stream disclosure: the checkbox isn't compliance

AD on Live's Oct 3 guide walks the FTC rules for streamers. Any material connection, free product included, needs clear disclosure inside the video itself, and on a live stream it has to repeat because viewers join midway. The safest single label is "Ad." Platform tools like Twitch Branded Content and YouTube's paid-promotion box are required but not sufficient, because the FTC holds the creator and the brand responsible. The practical stack is the checkbox, plus a continuous on-screen "Ad," plus spoken callouts.

Why this handle: Claims and disclosure hygiene for live brand deals, which is the Human Creative Director lane.

Source: adonlive.com/…/sponsorship-disclosure-live-streams (Oct 3) · ftc.gov endorsement guides

Brands, not just creators, are in the FTC crosshairs

Influencers Time (Oct 2) argues the current FTC endorsement wave names brands as co-respondents when they "knew or should have known" creators skipped clear #ad or Paid Partnership labels. The usual failures are disclosures below the fold, unused platform tags, scripted "organic" reviews and affiliate incentives. AI-generated testimonials and virtual influencers are explicitly in scope, so synthetic voices are no loophole. The ask for brands is to audit placement, not just contract language.

Why this handle: Brand-side sponsorship risk is exactly what a human creative director sells against.

Source: influencers-time.com/…/ftc-endorsement-sweep · ftc.gov
