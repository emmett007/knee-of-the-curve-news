The big one: one gene, 108 more days for old mice

The Medicine Maker covered the study yesterday. Image: The Medicine Maker

Turning old cells young has always had a nasty trade-off. Push the famous Yamanaka reprogramming factors too hard and a skin cell forgets it is a skin cell, which is how you get tumours. A team working out of Harvard's Wyss Institute tried a different route. They trained a machine-learning model on gene activity from 464 samples of human skin cells taken from donors of different ages. Then they used it to screen for single genes that make old cells read younger without losing their identity.

The winner was SRSF1, a gene that controls how a cell edits its own RNA messages. In old human skin cells, switching it up cut senescence, inflammatory signals and protein stress, and brought DNA repair programmes back. The cells still looked and behaved like skin cells. Then the team put a switchable copy of the gene into the livers of old female mice using a harmless virus. Median lifespan rose from 778 days to 886, which is 14%. Frailty built up about 44% more slowly, and the onset of lasting frailty came 123 days later. The paper ran in Nature Aging on Oct 14, and the coverage landed yesterday.

My read This is the cleanest "rejuvenation without the cancer risk" story of the month, and it gives LSN a clear sequel to the reprogramming question your audience keeps asking. It also shows AI doing real discovery work, which suits your crowd.

The catch It was 10 treated mice against 9 controls, all female. The benefit faded once the gene was switched off. Simply turning SRSF1 up as high as possible did not help skin repair or muscle regrowth, so timing and dose matter, and nobody yet knows exactly why it works. Say "a gene that made old mice live 14% longer." Don't say "a human anti-ageing therapy."

Sources: The Medicine Maker, Oct 19 · Nature Aging paper · Wyss Institute release

Short take: protect the blood-vessel lining, don't nuke it

Image: Lifespan Research Institute

A study in Aging Cell found that a protein called MTR4 guards the cells lining our blood vessels against DNA damage, and that it falls with age. Mice aged 20 months had much less of it than 3-month-old mice in the heart, liver and aorta. Switching MTR4 off in human vessel cells pushed them into senescence. Adding it back to worn-out cells partly reversed that, and the research summary says restoring it also lengthened lifespan in tiny C. elegans worms. The interesting part is the strategy. Earlier experiments that simply killed off senescent vessel cells caused scarring and shorter lives in mice, and in another case made high blood pressure worse. My read This is a good LSN companion to the SRSF1 story, because both are about making old cells tougher rather than destroying them. The catch It's cell and mouse work, with the lifespan gain shown only in worms.

Source: Lifespan Research Institute, Oct 19

Also on the wire: one hacker, an army of AI agents, 100 companies, $50 a break-in

Image: AI News Today

Reuters reported on Monday that a single Chinese-speaking hacker left almost all the work of a break-in campaign to AI agents. Starting Sept 25, the agents tried more than 1,200 online shops in the U.S., Canada, Australia and Japan, working from instructions shorter than 100 Chinese characters. They got into more than 100. A successful break-in took about 23 hours. The researcher who found it, Snorre Fagerland of NorForge, estimates the hacker spent about $5,000 on AI tokens, or roughly $50 per breach. The campaign reportedly used a mix of DeepSeek models and Anthropic's Claude Code. Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins were among the targets. Deere says it saw no sign that sensitive data was taken, SharkNinja says a standalone support site was hit and contained, and Cummins is investigating.

Two more pieces sit on the same shelf. Homeland Security has ordered every federal civilian agency to remove DeepSeek products, including apps built on its models, and the notice goes into the Federal Register today. It does not touch private use. And on X, "DeepInfer" is trending because DeepSeek open-sourced the engine that runs its services, while a separate benchmark story says its new V4.1 Flash model beat OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol at simulated corporate network intrusions, at a fraction of the cost.

My read This is a KOTC story with a dark punchline. Burglary used to need a crew; now it needs a credit card and a paragraph. The useful point for your audience is that small websites, the kind they own, are now worth an attacker's time because the attacker's time is nearly free.

The catch The breach details come from one security firm's research as reported by Reuters, and I read them through a news digest rather than the Reuters piece itself. The benchmark result is an X summary of a third-party test, not a peer-reviewed finding. Don't name the companies as "hacked" beyond what each one said.

Sources: AI News Today, Oct 20 (summarising Reuters and CyberScoop) · The AI Rankings daily debrief · X trend: DeepInfer · X story: DeepSeek V4.1 cyber benchmark

Podcasting today: Spotify opens paid memberships, and WTF comes back as a cartoon, sort of

Image: Spotify

Spotify launched Memberships this morning in 34 markets, including the U.S. Creators choose what members get: exclusive audio or video, bonus episodes, fewer ads, early access or the archive, across several price tiers. Fans join without leaving Spotify, and creators keep 90% of the revenue and can export member contact details when fans agree to share them. The bar to qualify is low: three published episodes, 100 listeners or viewers, and 100 hours of listening on Spotify in the last 30 days. The Shawn Ryan Show, Making Sense with Sam Harris and YMH Studios' 12 shows are among the first to switch it on.

Separately, Podnews reports that Marc Maron's WTF, which ended last October, has republished a two-hour 2011 episode with Norm Macdonald on YouTube in visual form. It's "animated, by humans," with photos and notes from the session laid over the audio rather than a full cartoon of the interview.

My read Memberships give Hollywood Podcasts a fresh sales line. A show that wants paid bonus episodes needs more content, and more content means more studio hours. The WTF piece is a Creative Director idea for OpusClip: old audio-only archives can be turned into watchable video without reshooting. The catch Spotify's own announcement is the only source for the terms, and the eligibility rules apply only to shows hosted on Spotify for Creators or Megaphone.

Sources: Spotify newsroom, Oct 20 · Podnews, Oct 20
