The big one

A $5,000 blood draw to bank tomorrow's immune cells

The kit is the product. The therapy is still a maybe. Image: Cellino via Athletech News

On Thursday, the team behind Cellino launched Reservoir: a consumer service that takes one standard blood draw, isolates your immune cells, and locks them below −150°C in an FDA-registered facility. The cells sit in your name. Release requires you and a licensed physician. The company line is blunt: Reservoir preserves cells. It does not treat.

My read: this is the longevity trade selling a freezer, not a drug. Cord-blood banks and egg freezers already sell cryopreservation; what is new is adult PBMCs as a consumer product with a clean custody chain. Emmett's LSN audience will ask the right question: what can a 2031 physician do with 2026 cells that a fresh draw could not? The honest answer today is "maybe nothing." The product is the bet that younger cells will matter.

The catch: no clinical claim, no trial readout, and five grand before you know whether the vault ever pays out. Cellino's FDA Advanced Manufacturing Technology badge covers its manufacturing platform — not Reservoir's preservation service. Do not confuse the parent's pedigree with the child's promise.

By the numbers:

  • $4,995: one-time price on withreservoir.com (draw, isolation, cryopreservation, first year of storage). Clinic pricing can vary.
  • $270 / year: storage after year one.
  • Below −150°C: storage temperature.
  • ~15 minutes / ~60 mL: draw time and volume. Adults 18+.
  • Over $96 million: Cellino venture funding to date; up to $25 million ARPA-H award to Cellino (not to Reservoir).

Sources: withreservoir.com · Athletech News · Business Wire

Also on the wire

The FTC just subpoenaed the two biggest AI labs

OpenAI's chief under the lights. Image: Getty Images via CBS News

On Wednesday the FTC sent civil investigative demands — paper trails, not fines — to OpenAI and Anthropic. Each demand runs more than fifty pages on safety incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy. An FTC spokesperson confirmed the move to CBS. OpenAI said it will engage constructively; Anthropic had no immediate comment in the wires.

My read: Tuesday JPMorgan locked outside agents out of the bank. Wednesday the regulator opened a discovery file on the labs that build them. Existing consumer-protection law is being aimed at agent escape without waiting for a new AI Act. Anyone running a bot staff should read this as the liability layer getting sharper, not as a product ban.

The catch: a CID is an evidence demand, not a finding. No complaint has been filed. Do not say the FTC sued anyone — it has not.

  • 50+ pages: length of each CID.
  • 2 firms named so far: OpenAI and Anthropic.
  • Wed Oct 14: day the demands went out.
  • 64%: share of U.S. adults who think AI is developing "too fast" (AP-NORC via CBS).

Sources: Axios · CBS News · Ars Technica
